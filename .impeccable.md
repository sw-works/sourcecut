## Design Context

### Users

Documentary producers, researchers, production designers, writers, and historical consultants use
SourceCut while turning primary-source research into defensible visual production decisions. Their
core job is to move quickly from a historical question to evidence, candidate archival references,
rights information, and exact source drill-down.

### Brand Personality

Modern, professional, creative. The interface should feel authoritative and composed without
becoming institutional or sterile. It should create confidence in provenance while retaining the
energy of a working filmmaker's research wall.

### Aesthetic Direction

Dark-theme editorial research desk: ink-black blue-tinted surfaces, warm archival paper tones, and
one restrained amber accent. Strong typographic hierarchy, asymmetrical editorial composition, and
real cached archival imagery should carry the visual identity. Avoid neon cyberpunk styling,
generic analytics dashboards, glassmorphism, purple-blue gradients, and chat-first layouts.

### Design Principles

1. Make provenance visible: every creative recommendation must lead naturally to exact evidence.
2. Lead with the research board, not application chrome or dashboard metrics.
3. Treat archival imagery and historical text as primary visual material, never decoration.
4. Use calm dark surfaces, disciplined typography, and amber only for meaningful focus or status.
5. Adapt the evidence timeline and board for touch and narrow screens without hiding core actions.
